## Study Notes

<!-- AI-GENERATED -->
*   **Hiddekel and Euphrates (*Chiddeqel*, H2313; *Perath*, H6578):** The last two rivers are the Tigris (Hiddekel) and the Euphrates — the rivers of Mesopotamia that frame the land later promised to Abraham (Gen 15:18). Hiddekel is named in Daniel's vision (Dan 10:4), and the Euphrates recurs throughout Scripture as the boundary of the land (Josh 1:4; Rev 9:14, 16:12). The Eden narrative's geography opens onto the geography of the covenant.
*   **"Goeth Toward the East of Assyria" (*halak*, H1980; *qidmah*, H6926; *Ashshur*, H804):** The Tigris flows "toward the east of Assyria" — the text orients the river against a real, known land. The precision of the geography (east of Assyria, land of Havilah, land of Cush) is the narrative's way of locating Eden in the world its first readers inhabited; the exact reconstruction of the pre-Flood landscape remains beyond the text's data.
*   **The Four Rivers as the Frame of the World (*arba'*, H702; *rosh*, H7218):** The four heads of the river (2:10) frame the known world: Havilah (gold), Cush (Ethiopia), Assyria, and the Euphrates. The symmetry is deliberate — the garden sits at the center of a world whose reaches are named. This is a textual observation about the narrative's structure.
