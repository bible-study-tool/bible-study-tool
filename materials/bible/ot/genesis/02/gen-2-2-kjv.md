## Study Notes

<!-- AI-GENERATED -->
*   **The First Rest (*shavat*, H7673):** Verse 2 records the first occurrence of *shavat* ("to cease, rest, desist") in Scripture — the verb from which "Sabbath" (*shabbat*) is named. God's rest is not the rest of exhaustion but the rest of *completion*: the work is finished, and the Creator ceases because nothing remains to be made. The verse's own framing — "from all his work which he had made" — ties the rest directly to the finished work.
*   **"God Ended His Work" (*kalah*, H3615) as Completion, Not Fatigue:** The repetition of *kalah* from 2:1 emphasizes that the seventh day begins with a *finished* cosmos. The text never suggests weariness; the LXX and later tradition (cf. John 5:17) understand God's continuing work as preservation, while the rest of Genesis 2 is the rest of the completed creation. This is a textual observation: the seventh day is the day of cessation after the six days of making.
*   **The Seventh Day (*yom shevi'i*, H3117 H7637):** The ordinal "seventh" recurs twice in this verse — "on the seventh day God ended his work... and he rested on the seventh day." The day is the goal toward which the six days moved; the creation week is ordered to its rest, not away from it. In the Adventist reading, this is the institution of the seventh-day Sabbath as a memorial of creation (Exod 20:11), presented here as the biblical basis, held within the framework of NOTICE.md.
