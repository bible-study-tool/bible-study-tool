## Study Notes

<!-- AI-GENERATED -->
*   **Naked and Not Ashamed (*arommim*, H6174; *bosh*, H954):** The chapter closes with the innocence of the original pair: "they were both naked, the man and his wife, and were not ashamed." The Hebrew *arommim* ("naked") shares its root with the word for the serpent's cunning in 3:1 (*arum*, "subtle" — the distinct entry H6175) — a wordplay the narrative deploys at the hinge of innocence and Fall. Nakedness without shame marks the pre-Fall state: no guilt, no hiding, no fear. The first shameful awareness of nakedness comes only after sin (3:7, 3:10-11).
*   **The Boundary of the Chapter:** Verse 25 ends the second creation account on the note of untroubled intimacy: the man and the woman, one flesh (2:24), naked and unashamed. The chapter's close sets the stage for the Fall narrative (ch. 3), where the serpent questions the command (2:16-17) and the pair's innocence is lost. The contrast is the narrative's own: the world as God made it, and the world after disobedience.
*   **The Innocence of the Original Order (a textual observation):** The verse presents the original human condition as one of openness and trust — no shame before each other and (implicitly) before God. The Adventist reading, with the text, sees in this the goodness of the created order that sin disrupted and redemption restores (Rev 3:18's clothing of shame; the new earth's restoration).
