## Study Notes

<!-- AI-GENERATED -->
*   **The Prohibition (*lo tokal*, H3808 H398):** "Thou shalt not eat of it" — the single negative command of the garden covenant. The tree of the knowledge of good and evil is withheld; every other tree is given. The prohibition is the boundary that defines the covenant: obedience is the trust that God's word is true and good. The narrative does not explain why the tree is forbidden beyond its name — the knowledge of good and evil belongs to God (3:5, 3:22) — and the Adventist reading, with the text, understands the test as one of faith in God's character.
*   **"Thou Shalt Surely Die" (*mut*, H4191):** The Hebrew *mot tamut* ("dying thou shalt die") is emphatic — the infinitive absolute construction. The threatened penalty is death: "in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die." The woman's recital (3:3) adds "lest ye die," and the serpent disputes it (3:4). The fulfillment unfolds in the narrative: the man and woman do not drop dead that day, yet death enters the world through sin (Rom 5:12; 6:23). In the Adventist understanding of conditional immortality (NOTICE.md), the threatened death is the cessation of the living being — the life that God's breath gave (2:7) is forfeited by sin, and eternal life is the gift of God (Rom 6:23). The interpretive care here is to read the penalty as the text defines it: the end of the living soul, not an immortal soul's torment.
*   **"In the Day" (*yom*, H3117):** The phrase "in the day that thou eatest" uses *yom* as in 2:4 — a construction marking the event as a whole ("when you eat"). The penalty is tied to the act, not to a calendar hour; the narrative's own pace (the pair live on, the serpent is judged, the ground cursed) shows the "day" as the day of the transgression itself.
