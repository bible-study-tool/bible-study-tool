## Study Notes

<!-- AI-GENERATED -->
*   **The First "Not Good" (*lo tov*, H3808 H2896):** For the first time in the creation narrative, something is pronounced "not good": "It is not good that the man should be alone." The refrain of ch. 1 ("God saw that it was good") is here inverted. The man's aloneness is the one deficiency of the original creation — and its remedy is the woman. Humanity is declared, from the beginning, to be relational: the image of God (1:27) is borne by male and female together.
*   **"An Help Meet for Him" (*ezer kenegdo*, H5828 H5048):** The phrase *ezer kenegdo* — "a help corresponding to him" / "meet for him" — is often misread as a subordinate helper. The word *ezer* ("help") is used elsewhere chiefly of God himself (Ps 121:1-2; 33:20), and *kenegdo* means "corresponding to, opposite to" — a counterpart, not a servant. The woman is the man's equal and complement: the only creature who corresponds to him. This is the foundational biblical text for the equal dignity of the sexes, consistent with the imago Dei of 1:27.
*   **The Making of the Woman (*asah*, H6213):** "I will make him an help meet" — the verb *asah* ("make") is the same used of the luminaries (1:16) and the firmament (1:7). The woman is a deliberate divine creation, not an afterthought. The narrative's care in presenting her (2:21-22) matches the care of 2:7's formation of the man.
