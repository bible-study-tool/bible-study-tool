## Study Notes

<!-- AI-GENERATED -->
*   **The Completion of the Creation Week (*kalah*, H3615):** "Thus the heavens and the earth were finished" (*wayekhullu*, from *kalah*, "to end, complete, finish"). The verb of completion closes the six working days that opened with the fiat of Genesis 1:1 — the same verb recurs in 2:2 ("God ended his work"). The creation narrative does not end with the sixth day's verdict alone; it ends with the finished cosmos as a whole, presented as a completed edifice.
*   **"All the Host of Them" (*tsaba*, H6635):** The word *tsaba* ("host, army") gathers the totality of what was made — the luminaries of Day 4, the creatures of Days 5-6 — as an ordered array. The same word later names the angelic host (1 Kings 22:19) and the Lord of hosts (YHWH Sabaoth); here it asserts that the entire created order, visible and invisible, stands as the finished work of God.
*   **The Bridge to the Seventh Day:** Verse 1 closes the six days and positions the reader at the threshold of the Sabbath. Genesis 1:31's "very good" verdict is now "finished"; what remains is the rest of 2:2-3. The structure deliberately builds toward the seventh day — the day that, in the Adventist reading, is the crown of creation week (see 2:2-3).
