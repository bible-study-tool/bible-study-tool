*   **The Opened Eyes of Guilt (*va-tipakakhnah eyney shneyhem*):** The serpent had promised opened eyes leading to divine wisdom (v. 5). The promise was fulfilled only in bitter irony: their eyes were opened not to heavenly majesty, but to their own destitution and guilt: *va-yede'u ki eyrummim hem* ("and they knew that they were naked"). Notice that the word for nakedness here is *eyrom* (H5903), distinct from the *arom* (H6174) of 2:25, underscoring the shift from innocent unclad purity to conscious, shameful exposure.
*   **The Departure of the Garment of Light:** In Adventist Edenic theology (drawing from Ellen White in *Patriarchs and Prophets*, p. 57), unfallen Adam and Eve were clothed with a robe of light and glory—the celestial garment worn by the angels and reflecting the divine presence. Disobedience severed communion with God, causing this radiant garment to fade instantly. Chilled and conscious of their nakedness, the fallen pair sought an earthly substitute.
*   **Fig Leaves as the Prototype of Self-Righteousness (*aleh te'enah*):** The sewing together of fig leaves (*aleh te'enah*, H5929/H8384) to make aprons or girdles (*chagorot*, H2290) represents humanity's inaugural effort at self-justification: attempting by human contrivance to cover moral transgression and conceal guilt. As the subsequent narrative proves (v. 8–10), the fig leaves failed to alleviate their terror when God approached. True covering cannot be manufactured by human hands; it requires divine provision through the sacrifice of the Lamb (Gen 3:21; Rev 3:18).
