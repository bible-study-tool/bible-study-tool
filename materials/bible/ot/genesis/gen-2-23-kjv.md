## Study Notes

<!-- AI-GENERATED -->
*   **The First Human Speech (*ishshah*, H802; *ish*, H376):** Verse 23 records the first words of the man in Scripture — and they are a poem of recognition: "This is now bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh." The Hebrew wordplay is the point: *ish* ("man") names her *ishshah* ("woman") — "she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man." The naming is one of celebration and equality: the woman is the man's own flesh, his counterpart, not a stranger and not a subordinate.
*   **"Bone of My Bones, and Flesh of My Flesh" (*etsem*, H6106; *basar*, H1320):** The idiom of "bone and flesh" expresses the closest kinship in Hebrew (cf. Gen 29:14; Judg 9:2 — "ye are my bone and my flesh"). The man recognizes the woman as his own kind: of the same substance, the same family. The phrase is the narrative's own gloss on the meaning of the rib — the woman is not a separate species but the man's equal partner.
*   **The Pattern of the Pair (*echad*, H259):** The man's words here prepare 2:24's "one flesh": the two are one because the woman was taken from the man. The creation of the pair is the foundation of marriage as Scripture presents it — the union of equals, ordained in Eden. This is the text the Lord cites in Matthew 19:4-5 and Paul echoes in Ephesians 5:31.
