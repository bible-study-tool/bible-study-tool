## Study Notes

<!-- AI-GENERATED -->
*   **The Mist (*ed*, H108):** The Hebrew *ed* ("mist, fog") is a hapax-like word of debated meaning (some render "stream" or "flood"). In the KJV's reading, a mist rose from the earth and watered its whole face — the pre-rain irrigation of 2:5, rising from below rather than falling from above. The exact nature of this watering is uncertain in scholarship; the text's point is that the original earth was watered by divine provision.
*   **"Went Up... and Watered" (*alah*, H5927; *shaqah*, H8248):** The verb pair — rising and watering — depicts a self-sustaining cycle: the mist ascends from the earth and waters it. The same verb *shaqah* recurs for the rivers of Eden (2:10) and for the garden's irrigation. Creation is depicted as a closed, life-giving system under God's design.
*   **Creation's Hydrology as Divine Order:** Together with 2:5, verse 6 describes the pre-Fall water cycle — no rain, no man, yet the earth watered. The orderly provision anticipates the garden of 2:8-9: God prepares the environment before placing the man. This is a textual observation, not a scientific claim.
