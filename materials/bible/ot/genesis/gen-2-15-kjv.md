## Study Notes

<!-- AI-GENERATED -->
*   **"To Dress It and to Keep It" (*abad*, H5647; *shamar*, H8104):** The man's vocation is summed in two verbs: *abad* ("to serve, work, till") and *shamar* ("to keep, guard, watch"). These are the verbs later used of Israel's service to God (Num 3:7-8) and of keeping the commandments (Deut 4:2). The garden is not a leisure park: the man is its worker and its guardian. The creation mandate of 1:28 (subdue, have dominion) is here specified as *cultivation and protection* — the original form of dominion is stewardship, not exploitation. This is the biblical basis of the Adventist environmental-stewardship reading.
*   **The Placement (*yanach*, H3240; *sum*, H7760):** "The LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden" — the verb *yanach* ("to rest, set down, deposit") together with the taking suggests a deliberate placement: the man is set in his vocation by God. The garden is the man's workplace and home, the place of his blessing.
*   **Work Before the Fall:** The pre-Fall world includes work — but work as blessing, not burden. The curse later makes the ground's yield laborious (3:17-19); here the man's labour is joy. The Sabbath rest of 2:2-3 comes *after* six days of work (1:1-2:1), and the garden vocation gives the work its shape. This is a textual observation on the goodness of work in the original order.
