# Genesis 1:3 - KJV

> And God said, Let there be light: and there was light.

## Hebrew Word Study

### amar (to say) - Strong's H559

*   Transliteration: amar
    
*   Definition: to say, speak, command, declare
    
*   Usage Count: about 2450 times in the OT
    
*   Context: in the creation narrative "God said" carries the force of a divine decree - God speaks and it happens.
    

### yehi (let there be) - Strong's H1961

*   Transliteration: yehi (jussive of haya)
    
*   Definition: let it be, let there be, become
    
*   Usage Count: used repeatedly in Genesis 1 (verses 3, 6, 9, 11, 14, 20, 22, 24, 26, 28-30)
    
*   Note: the jussive "let there be" expresses a divine decree, immediately fulfilled ("and there was").
    

### or (light) - Strong's H216

*   Transliteration: or
    
*   Definition: light
    
*   Usage Count: about 250 times in the OT
    
*   Note: Hebrew "or" is the noun "light" in Gen 1:3, Strong's H216. The earlier entry also referenced the related verb root H215, which is not the word used here. Keep only H216 (the noun) tagged for Gen 1:3.
    

### Day 1 light vs the heavenly lights

The "light" created on Day 1 (H216) is distinct from the lights appointed on Day 4 (Gen 1:14-18). This is a notable interpretive point.

## Cross-Language and Study Notes

*   The Hebrew "yehi" (let there be) parallels the Greek "genethto" (let it be / let there come to be), both jussive-imperative forms expressing divine decree.
    
*   The Hebrew "or" (light) corresponds to the Greek "phos" (light), a direct semantic equivalence with English "light".
    
*   "Light before the sun" (Day 1 light vs Day 4 luminaries) is an interpretive emphasis - one position among others, not the plain reading.
    
*   In Adventist study the Day 1 light has traditionally been read as the presence/glory of God, but this is an interpretive tradition and must be presented with nuance, not as settled fact.

## AI Summary

Genesis 1:3 is the first divine creative speech: God speaks, and light comes to be. The Hebrew "yehi" (let there be) shows creation as God's commanding word. The "light" (H216) here is distinct from the Day-4 heavenly bodies, and in some traditions is identified with God's glory. This sets the pattern for the creation week. The connection to John 1:3 and Hebrews 11:3 links the creative word to the Word (Logos), central to Adventist Christology.
