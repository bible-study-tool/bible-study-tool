## Study Notes

<!-- AI-GENERATED -->
*   **The Completed Naming (*qara*, H7121):** "Adam gave names to all cattle, and to the fowl of the air, and to every beast of the field" — the naming of 2:19 is completed across the three classes of the animal creation (the same tripartite order as 1:24-25). The man's first work — the ordering of the animal world by name — is finished. It is the one work the man performs before the woman is made: a task he does alone, and whose completion reveals his aloneness.
*   **"There Was Not Found an Help Meet" (*matza*, H4672; *ezer kenegdo*, H5828 H5048):** The search concludes: among every named creature, no counterpart is found. The animals — formed from the same ground, brought before the man, named by him — are not his equal partner. The negative result is the narrative's preparation for 2:21-22: the helper must come from the man himself, not from the animal world. The phrase *ezer kenegdo* (see 2:18) marks the woman as the corresponding equal the animals cannot be.
*   **The Man's Aloneness as the Setting of the Creation of Woman:** The sequence of the narrative (the naming, the search, the not-found) presents the woman's creation as the answer to a real need, not a divine afterthought. The man's solitude is the one "not good" of creation (2:18), and the woman is its remedy. This is the narrative's own structure; the theological reading of the pair's equality follows from it (see 2:18, 2:23).
