## Study Notes

<!-- AI-GENERATED -->
*   **"God Made" (*wayya'as*, H6213) — Creatures, Not Gods:** The verb *asah* ("to make") places the luminaries in the same class as the rest of the created order; they are fashioned things, not deities to be served. The Hebrew text never names the sun (*shemesh*) or the moon (*yareach*) — the divine names used throughout the ancient Near East. Genesis 1 calls them only "the greater light" and "the lesser light" (*ma'or* H3974), reducing the feared astral deities of surrounding nations to functional light-bearers.
*   **The H853 Object Markers the English Never Shows:** The Hebrew marks the objects of *wayya'as* with four occurrences of the particle *et* (H853) — the two great lights, the greater light, the lesser light, and the stars — under the single verb of divine making. English has no equivalent for this definite-object marker, and scrollmapper KJV-osis omits all four H853 tags (documented in `correlations/apparatus-genesis1.json`, key `Gen.1.16`). The apparatus row is the textual hook: the pairing "the greater light... the lesser light" stands in the Hebrew as marked, distinct created objects under the one divine verb.
*   **"He Made the Stars Also" (H3556):** The seemingly incidental trailing clause. In the ancient world the stars were divine beings who determined fate; Genesis disposes of the entire starry host in five words. The stars are not the focus of worship — they are creatures, made "also," on the same day and by the same hand.
*   **Rule as Service (*memshalah*, H4475):** The two great lights are appointed "to rule" — but their rule is exercised by giving light (v15, v17-18). Dominion in the creation order is stewardship and service, the pattern later conferred on humanity (v26-28) and, in Christian reading, supremely exemplified in Christ (cf. Mark 10:45).
