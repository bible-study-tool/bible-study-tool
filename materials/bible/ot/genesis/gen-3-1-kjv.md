## Study Notes

<!-- AI-GENERATED -->
*   **The Subtle Serpent (*nachash*, H5175; *arum*, H6175):** The chapter opens with an immediate thematic and verbal contrast to the close of chapter 2. Genesis 2:25 described unfallen humanity as naked (*arummim*, H6174) and unashamed; Genesis 3:1 introduces the serpent as *arum* (H6175, "subtil", crafty, prudent). This wordplay highlights the vulnerability of open innocence before predatory craftiness. The serpent is designated a creature "which the LORD God had made," but Christian and Adventist theology, following Revelation 12:9 and 20:2, recognizes that Satan used the serpent as a disguise and medium to deceive humanity.
*   **Questioning the Divine Command (*af ki-amar Elohim*):** The assault begins not with blatant denial, but with an insinuation that undermines trust in God's goodness: "Yea, hath God said, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?" The serpent inverts God's generous charter in 2:16 ("Of every tree of the garden thou mayest freely eat") into an austere and unreasonable restriction. Furthermore, while the narrative uses the covenant name "the LORD God" (*YHWH Elohim* in v. 1a), the serpent omits *YHWH* and speaks only of the generic *Elohim* (v. 1b), distancing the personal covenant bond between Creator and creature.
*   **The Adventist Theological Framework:** In the Great Controversy theme (NOTICE.md), the Fall centers upon a moral controversy over God's character and government. Satan sought to present God as arbitrary, severe, and withholding good from His creatures. Entertaining dialogue with the tempter rather than resting upon the explicit declaration of God proved fatal to the pair's safety.
