## Study Notes

<!-- AI-GENERATED -->
*   **Adding to the Word of God (*lo tiggu bo*):** In Genesis 2:17, God's explicit instruction was "thou shalt not eat of it." Eve adds an additional restriction: *ve-lo tiggu bo* ("neither shall ye touch it", from *naga*, H5060). In biblical wisdom literature, augmenting God's word is severely warned against (Prov 30:6; Rev 22:18). Creating a human fence around God's law proved a fatal misstep: once the woman was near enough to gaze upon and touch the tree without dying, the certainty of the divine prohibition collapsed.
*   **Diluting the Penalty (*pen-temutun*):** The divine warning in 2:17 used the emphatic infinitive absolute *mot tamut* ("dying thou shalt die" / "thou shalt surely die"), denoting absolute certainty. Eve softens this to *pen-temutun* ("lest ye die", from *pen*, H6435, expressing contingency or possibility). By transforming an immutable consequence into a mere hazard, the woman weakened her defense, inviting the serpent's direct denial in verse 4.
*   **"The Tree in the Midst of the Garden":** Notice that Eve avoids the full title "tree of the knowledge of good and evil" (2:17), referring to it instead by its location: *ha-ets asher be-tokh ha-gan* ("the tree which is in the midst of the garden").
