## Study Notes

*   **The Threefold Anatomy of Temptation:** The text details the internal progression of desire within the woman, foreshadowing the classic apostolic triad in 1 John 2:16:
    1. *Tov ha-ets le-ma'akhal* ("the tree was good for food") corresponds to physical appetite / the lust of the flesh.
    2. *Ta'avah hu la-eynayim* ("pleasant to the eyes", *ta'avah*, H8378) corresponds to sensory and aesthetic delight / the lust of the eyes.
    3. *Nechmad ha-ets le-haskil* ("desired to make one wise", *chamad*, H2530; *sakal*, H7919) corresponds to intellectual ambition / the pride of life.
    Satan’s strategy targets the physical senses, the aesthetic faculties, and the intellect, leading the mind to substitute self-fulfillment for reliance upon divine revelation.
*   **The Decisive Verbs of Transgression:** The rapid succession of consecutive verbs marks the transition from contemplation to outward rebellion: she *saw* (*va-tere*), she *took* (*va-tikakh*), she *ate* (*va-tokhal*), and she *gave* (*va-titten*). Temptation unresisted inevitably materializes in physical action.
*   **Adam's Deliberate Transgression (*gam le-ishah immah va-yokhal*):** The text records that she "gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat." The New Testament emphasizes that "Adam was not deceived, but the woman being deceived was in the transgression" (1 Tim 2:14). In the Adventist understanding (NOTICE.md), Adam was not deluded by the serpent's speech, but acted out of sorrowful attachment to his wife, choosing human love above divine allegiance. Consequently, Romans 5:12–19 identifies Adam as the covenantal representative whose deliberate disobedience plunged humanity into the fallen state.
