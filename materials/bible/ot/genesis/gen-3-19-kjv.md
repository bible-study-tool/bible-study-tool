*   **The Culmination of the Sentence (*ki-afar attah ve-el-afar tashuv*):** The climax of God's judgment over Adam concludes with the solemn declaration of mortality: "for dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return" (*ki-afar attah ve-el-afar tashuv*). This sentence ratifies the warning of Genesis 2:17: "in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die."
*   **Biblical Wholism and Conditional Immortality (NOTICE.md):** In Seventh-day Adventist anthropology, Genesis 3:19 is a cornerstone text for understanding human nature and the state of the dead. In Genesis 2:7, human life was the composite union of dust from the ground (*afar min-ha-adamah*) and the divine breath of life (*nishmat chayyim*). Here, death is defined not as the liberation of an immortal soul to another realm of consciousness, but as the dissolution of the living person: the breath of life returns to God and the physical organism returns to dust (Eccl 12:7; Ps 104:29; Ps 146:4). Humans possess no inherent immortality; immortality is an attribute of God alone (1 Tim 6:16), which believers receive conditionally as a gift of grace at the resurrection (1 Cor 15:52–54).
*   **The Sweat of the Face (*be-ze'at appekha*):** Until the grave is reached, human existence is marked by struggle: "In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat bread (*be-ze'at appekha tokhal lechem*), till thou return unto the ground." Work in Eden was joyful stewardship; post-Fall labor is accompanied by weariness, exhaustion, and the inevitable downward pull toward physical cessation.
