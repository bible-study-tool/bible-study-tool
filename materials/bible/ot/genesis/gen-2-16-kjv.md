## Study Notes

<!-- AI-GENERATED -->
*   **The Command (*tsavah*, H6680):** "The LORD God commanded the man" — the first divine command addressed directly to humanity. The verb *tsavah* ("to command, charge, enjoin") is the language of covenant and law (cf. Deut 4:2: "ye shall not add unto the word which I command you"). The man receives the command as a covenant partner: he is addressed, not merely made.
*   **"Of Every Tree... Thou Mayest Freely Eat" (*akal*, H398):** The command opens with generous permission: "of every tree of the garden thou mayest freely eat" (the infinitive absolute *akol tokel*, "eating thou mayest eat" — emphatic). The prohibition of 2:17 is set inside a frame of abundance. The Adventist reading notes the same pattern in 1:29: the Creator's first word to humanity is provision, and the one restriction defines the boundary of trust (see 2:17).
*   **The Garden as Gift and Test:** The command situates the man in a garden of delights with a single boundary. The structure — free access to every tree, one tree withheld — makes the man's obedience meaningful: love and trust are tested by the one thing not given. This is the narrative's own design, developed at 3:1-6.
