## Study Notes

<!-- AI-GENERATED -->
*   **The Blessing of the Seventh Day (*barak*, H1288):** God *blesses* the seventh day — the only day of creation week so marked. The blessing of the day echoes the blessings spoken over the creatures (1:22, 1:28); a day, not merely a creature, is here the object of divine blessing. The Sabbath is thus not an afterthought or a burden but a gift — blessed by the Creator before any human existed to observe it.
*   **The Sanctification of the Seventh Day (*qadash*, H6942):** God *sanctifies* the seventh day — sets it apart as holy. The verb *qadash* ("to be holy, consecrate") is the language of the sanctuary and the priesthood; the seventh day is the first thing in Scripture called holy. The blessing and sanctification together make the day the enduring memorial of creation — later restated as the fourth commandment (Exod 20:8-11) and observed by Christ (Mark 2:27-28).
*   **The Seventh Day and the Divine Rest (*shavat*, H7673):** The causal clause — "because that in it he had rested from all his work" — grounds the blessing in the rest of 2:2. The day is blessed *because* God rested in it. The Adventist reading holds this as the origin of the seventh-day Sabbath: a creation ordinance, blessed and sanctified before sin, kept by Christ and the apostles (Luke 4:16; Acts 17:2), and observed in the new earth (Isa 66:23) — presented here as the confessional framework per NOTICE.md, with the biblical basis shown.
