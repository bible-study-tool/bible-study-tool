# Historical Preamble — The Gospel of John

## Book Overview

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Author** | John the Apostle (son of Zebedee; "the disciple whom Jesus loved") |
| **Date of writing** | Circa AD 85–95 (late first century, likely from Ephesus) |
| **Original audience** | Diverse, Hellenistic audience — Jewish and Gentile believers; possibly a counter to early Gnostic tendencies |
| **Language** | Koine Greek |
| **Literary genre** | Theological biography (Gospel); highly theological and symbolic |
| **Central purpose** | *"That ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing ye might have life through his name."* (John 20:31) |

## Historical Era

John wrote during the **late Second Temple period's aftermath** — after the destruction of Jerusalem and the Temple by Rome in AD 70. The Jewish-Christian community was under pressure from both Rome and from Jewish synagogue expulsion (cf. John 9:22; 16:2). The apostle himself was exiled to the island of Patmos (Revelation 1:9) before writing from Ephesus.

## Political-Cultural Setting

- **Roman occupation**: The entire narrative takes place under Roman imperial administration. Pontius Pilate's authority (John 18–19), the threat of Roman reprisal ("lest the Romans shall come and take away our place and nation," John 11:48), and crucifixion as a Roman capital punishment are all central to understanding the passion narrative.
- **Jewish religious structure**: The Sanhedrin (ruling council of 70 elders + high priest) held civil and religious authority over Jews. The Pharisees and chief priests competed for influence. The high-priestly role was **politically appointed** by Rome — Caiaphas held it from AD 18–36 — making his office as corrupt as it was official.
- **Feast calendar**: John structures the entire Gospel around Jewish feasts (Passover × 3, Tabernacles, Dedication/Hanukkah). Understanding these feasts is essential: Jesus's actions at each feast are deliberate theological declarations.
- **Synagogue expulsion** (*aposynagōgos*, John 9:22; 12:42; 16:2): By the late first century, Jewish Christians were being formally expelled from synagogues — a social and economic catastrophe in the ancient world, not merely a religious inconvenience.

## Geographical Context

| Location | Significance |
|---|---|
| **Galilee** (Capernaum, Cana, Sea of Galilee) | Jesus's base of ministry; despised by Judean religious elite ("Can any good thing come out of Nazareth?" — John 1:46) |
| **Judea / Jerusalem** | Center of Temple worship and political power; where the conflict escalates |
| **Samaria** (Sychar, Jacob's Well) | Jews and Samaritans had 600 years of ethnic-religious hostility; Jesus's dialogue with the Samaritan woman (John 4) was shocking to all witnesses |
| **Bethany** | Village 2 miles east of Jerusalem; home of Mary, Martha, and Lazarus |
| **Beyond the Jordan** | John the Baptist's ministry location; safe refuge for Jesus (John 10:40) |
| **Ephesus** (author's location) | Major Hellenistic city in Asia Minor; gateway between Greek philosophy and Eastern religion |

## Key Cultural Keys for Reading John

1. **"The Jews"** (*hoi Ioudaioi*): In John, this phrase most often refers specifically to the **Jerusalem religious leadership** (Pharisees, chief priests, Sanhedrin), not to the Jewish people as a whole — who were themselves largely Jewish. Misreading this has caused immense harm; read it with precision.
2. **Night as shame**: Nicodemus comes "by night" (John 3:2) — this was not merely a time detail. Night visits by prominent men meant avoiding public association. Jesus's patient reception is all the more striking.
3. **Foot-washing** (John 13): Done by the lowest-ranking slave, never by an equal, and never by a superior. Jesus taking up a slave's towel was culturally incomprehensible to the disciples — the object lesson of servant-leadership could not have been more visceral.
4. **"I AM"** (*egō eimi*): John records seven great "I AM" statements (6:35, 8:12, 10:9, 10:11, 11:25, 14:6, 15:1) plus the absolute "I AM" (8:58) echoing the divine name of Exodus 3:14. Hearers recognized this as the divine name.
5. **The Logos** (John 1:1): *Logos* was both a Jewish (Wisdom of God, cf. Proverbs 8) and Greek philosophical concept (the rational principle ordering the cosmos). John uses it deliberately to address both audiences simultaneously.

## Spirit of Prophecy on John's Gospel

> *"The last of the apostles to remain alive, John had outlived most of his fellow disciples. He had seen Jerusalem destroyed and its temple in ruins. He had seen Christians tortured and killed for their faith... Out of this experience came the Gospel of John."*  
> — *Ellen G. White, The Acts of the Apostles*, chapter 55 (AA 539–557)

> *"The last of the twelve apostles, he outlived all the rest... He lived to behold the wide spread of the gospel and to see the churches he had helped to establish grow and flourish."*  
> — *Acts of the Apostles*, p. 570
